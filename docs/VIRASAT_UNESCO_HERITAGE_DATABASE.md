# VIRASAT UNESCO World Heritage Master Database

## 1. Executive Summary
The **VIRASAT UNESCO World Heritage Master Database** provides comprehensive, authoritative documentation for all **42 officially inscribed UNESCO World Heritage Properties** in the Republic of India as of 2026. India possesses the 6th highest number of World Heritage properties globally, representing millennia of continuous civilization, monumental architecture, and extraordinary biodiversity.

## 3. UNESCO Selection Criteria Reference

Properties are inscribed based on ten strict evaluation criteria established under the 1972 World Heritage Convention:

### Cultural Criteria
- **(i)**: Masterpiece of human creative genius (e.g., Taj Mahal, Sun Temple Konark, Ellora Caves).
- **(ii)**: Exhibit an important interchange of human values over a span of time (e.g., Qutb Minar, Mahabalipuram).
- **(iii)**: Unique or exceptional testimony to a cultural tradition or civilization (e.g., Hampi, Khajuraho, Dholavira).
- **(iv)**: Outstanding example of a type of building, architectural ensemble, or landscape (e.g., Brihadisvara Temple, Fatehpur Sikri).
- **(v)**: Outstanding example of traditional human settlement or land-use (e.g., Western Ghats agricultural landscapes).
- **(vi)**: Directly or tangibly associated with events, living traditions, ideas, or artistic works (e.g., Mahabodhi Temple, Bodh Gaya).

### Natural Criteria
- **(vii)**: Superlative natural phenomena or areas of exceptional natural beauty (e.g., Great Himalayan National Park, Khangchendzonga).
- **(viii)**: Outstanding examples representing major stages of earth's history and geological processes.
- **(ix)**: Outstanding examples representing significant ongoing ecological and biological processes (e.g., Sundarbans, Kaziranga).
- **(x)**: Most important and significant natural habitats for in-situ conservation of biological diversity (e.g., Manas, Keoladeo, Western Ghats).

## 5. Architectural & Ecological Significance
- **Ancient Civilizational Engineering**: Dholavira exhibits the world's most sophisticated proto-historic water harvesting systems dating back to 3000 BCE.
- **Rock-Cut Cave Art**: Ajanta, Ellora, and Elephanta preserve unprecedented achievements in sculpture and tempera painting.
- **Sacred Dravidian & Vesara Geometry**: The Great Living Chola Temples and the Hoysala Ensembles of Belur, Halebidu, and Somanathapura showcase zenith stone carving mastery.
- **Biodiversity Hotspots**: The Western Ghats and Sundarbans represent global biodiversity hotspots harboring critically endangered endemic species.
